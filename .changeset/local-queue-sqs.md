The local queue works like SQS and the Lambda event source mapping deployed for it, and runs each batch the way the queue runtime does. A message waits for its delay, stays invisible for the visibility timeout (the service `timeout`, 150 s by default) on each receive, expires at the end of the retention, and moves to the dead-letter queue after `deadLetter.maxAttempts` receives; without a dead-letter queue a failing message is retried until it expires. A failed message comes back after the runtime's backoff (`backoff`, 5 to 30 s) and `retry()` counts as an attempt, so `attempt` and `maxAttempts` read as in production. FIFO and fair queues require the group; FIFO queues deduplicate by `uniqueId`, or by content, for five minutes within a group, and a group delivers in order, holding its later messages after a failure. Subscriptions take batches up to `batch` (10 by default), wait for the batching window on standard queues, run at most `concurrency` batches and `parallelism` records at once, report a timeout a second before the deadline and hand unfinished records back at it. Sends are checked as SQS checks them: 1 MiB counting the trace attributes, a delay up to 900 s and none on FIFO queues, group and deduplication ids of 1 to 128 printable characters. `receiveMessage` works, with long polling. An imported queue keeps the order of its messages, forwards the delay, retries while the owner project is unavailable, and fails the send where production fails it. Each record runs in a scope of its own. `QueueTester.getDeadLetterMessages` and `QueueTester.waitForDrain` inspect a local queue. `ez4 run` stops the subscriptions when its scripts end, so a delayed message a script sends is no longer delivered. Run the consumer's suite before upgrading: messages now reach the handler in batches and with retries, and a spec that waited a fixed time for a delivery may need `QueueTester.waitForDrain`.
